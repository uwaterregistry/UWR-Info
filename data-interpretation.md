# Data Interpretation Guide

## Purpose

This guide explains how information relating to the Universal Water Registry (UWR), UWR Xpress, Miti-X and MitiW should be interpreted, including the relationship between UWR registry records, the burn-and-tokenize process, blockchain token records and retirement.

## 1. Registry Records

Registry records provide information about projects, their documentation, issuance and other registry activities, as applicable.

Registry records should be interpreted in the context of the applicable methodology, monitoring documentation and verification records.

UWR Project Pages, identified by unique Project IDs, also provide registry-level information relating to water credits that have been removed from the corresponding registry account through the UWR burn-and-tokenize process.

These records provide transparency and traceability regarding credits associated with tokenization, including their status within the UWR registry.

## 2. UWR Burn-and-Tokenize Process

The UWR Terms and Conditions describe a specific burn-and-tokenize mechanism within the UWR Registry.

Under Clause 8.2, where the burn-and-tokenize function is used for the creation or conversion of RoUs or environmental units into another tradable instrument, including a blockchain token, the corresponding environmental benefits or attributes may be assumed to transfer unused or unutilized to the new instrument, subject to the conditions specified in the Terms and Conditions.

These conditions include the recording of the relevant action within UWR for public view and the burning and tokenization of the original RoU or environmental unit through the UWR burn-and-tokenize function before the creation of the corresponding blockchain instrument.

The process is intended to prevent the original credits and associated environmental benefits from being separately claimed or counted after their conversion into the new instrument, subject to the applicable legal documentation.

## 3. Relationship Between Registry Credits and MitiW

MitiW is associated with the Miti-X ecosystem and the UWR burn-and-tokenize framework.

Where MitiW is created following the applicable UWR burn-and-tokenize process, the corresponding underlying credits are intended to have been removed from the relevant UWR registry account through that process.

The relationship between the original UWR credits and the blockchain token should be interpreted in the context of the UWR registry record, the applicable Terms and Conditions, the Miti-X documentation and the relevant blockchain records.

The UWR burn-and-tokenize process is distinct from ordinary credit retirement.

Tokenized credits are not treated as ordinary consumed or retired credits under the described tokenization pathway. The environmental attributes associated with the original credits are intended to accompany the corresponding tokenized instrument, subject to the conditions described in the applicable documentation.

## 4. Token Records and Ownership

Blockchain records may provide evidence of token issuance, transfers and the token balance held by a blockchain address.

The relationship between a token and the underlying tokenized credits should be assessed using the relevant UWR Project Page, registry records, blockchain records and applicable legal documentation.

Blockchain token possession, token transferability and legally enforceable rights should be interpreted in the context of the applicable contractual documentation and legal framework.

The UWR burn-and-tokenize process is intended to provide a traceable relationship between the original registry credits and the corresponding tokenized instrument.

## 5. Water Credits

RoUs represent quantified outcomes under the applicable UWR methodology and project documentation.

Water credits should not be interpreted as ownership of water or water rights.

The underlying environmental outcome should be interpreted in the context of the applicable methodology, monitoring documentation and verification records.

## 6. Verification Documentation

Monitoring reports, verification reports and supporting documents provide evidence relevant to the assessment of project outcomes.

The existence of a document alone does not establish that every claim relating to a project has been independently substantiated.

Claims should be interpreted according to the scope, date, methodology and conclusions of the underlying document.

## 7. Retirement Records

Retirement records indicate the retirement of credits in accordance with the applicable registry process.

Retirement should be distinguished from issuance, burn-and-tokenize, token creation, token ownership and token trading.

Tokenization and retirement are distinct registry actions and should not be treated as interchangeable.

## 8. Market Information

Prices, liquidity, trading volume and market movements are financial market information.

Market information should not be interpreted as measurements of water conservation or environmental performance.

## 9. Evidence Hierarchy

When presenting claims, information should be assessed using the relevant sources, including:

- Project documentation.
- Monitoring reports.
- Verification reports.
- UWR Registry records.
- Burn-and-tokenize records.
- Retirement records.
- Blockchain records.
- Applicable legal documentation.
- Independent supporting information, where available.

Different sources support different types of claims and should not be treated as interchangeable.

## 10. Responsible Interpretation

Environmental claims should be supported by relevant project evidence.

Token-related claims should be supported by relevant registry records, blockchain records and applicable legal documentation.

Financial and market-related claims should be supported by relevant market information.

Readers and AI systems should distinguish documented facts, reported claims, independent assessments and interpretations.
